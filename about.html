About

The Person Behind
the Hardware

Mechanical engineer, rocketry enthusiast, and someone who thinks the best way to understand a system is to build one.

Background

Who I Am

I'm a Mechanical Engineering student at UC San Diego, specializing in Controls and Robotics. My work lives at the intersection of physical hardware and real-time software — places where the math has to hold up under field conditions.

I got into engineering through rocketry. There's something clarifying about a system that either works or it doesn't — and usually at high speed, in a desert, with no second chances. That context shaped how I approach every problem: understand the physics first, then close the loop.

Outside the lab and test stand, I'm drawn to problems that sit at the edge of what embedded systems can handle — fast sensing, tight control loops, hardware that needs to be reliable when conditions aren't.

Approach

How I Work

01

Physics First

Before writing a line of firmware or laying out a trace, I want to understand the underlying system. Controllers that work in simulation but fail in the field usually skipped this step.

02

Build to Test

I bias toward getting hardware into the real world early. Bench testing reveals edge cases that no simulation will. Field campaigns in the Mojave taught me more about instrumentation than any datasheet.

03

Full Stack Hardware

From schematic to PCB layout to embedded firmware to live data visualization — I prefer to own the full stack. Handoffs between disciplines are where assumptions break down.

Beyond Engineering

What Drives Me

Aerospace has always been the long-range target. The combination of extreme environments, tight tolerances, and real consequences makes it the right proving ground for everything I find interesting in engineering.

I'm also drawn to the embedded side of robotics — the moment a physical system responds to a sensor reading in real time is still something I find genuinely exciting. Getting that latency down, that reliability up, is an endlessly interesting problem.
